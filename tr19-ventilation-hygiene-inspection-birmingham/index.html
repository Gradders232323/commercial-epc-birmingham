01 · Scope and preparation

Distinguish air systems from grease extract

TR19 Air concerns ventilation-system hygiene. Kitchen extraction with grease contamination is addressed through a different TR19 Grease context. State which systems are involved; do not assume a general ventilation inspection is a kitchen fire-risk assessment.

02 · Scope and preparation

Map the system and its access

Provide duct drawings where available, the rooms served, access-panel information, previous inspection or cleaning reports and any concerns about contamination. An inspection of accessible areas cannot establish the condition of concealed sections that have not been examined.

03 · Scope and preparation

Agree evidence and follow-up work

Confirm the inspection method, areas included and form of reporting in the quotation. Cleaning, access alterations, remedial work and post-clean verification should be identified separately where needed. The use of a technical standard does not establish that a business holds a particular certification.

Birmingham property context

Plant, served areas and safe access

For office or industrial accommodation, identify the rooms each system serves and who controls the equipment. Explain shared roofs, external plant areas and any restricted access rather than assuming one site contact can open every area.

Before you enquire

TR19 Ventilation Hygiene Inspections: useful answers

Is this automatically a duct-cleaning service?

No. Inspection, cleaning and verification are distinct activities. Tell us which outcomes you need so the scope can be agreed.

Does it cover kitchen extraction?

Mention kitchen extraction explicitly. Grease-extract risks need the appropriate scope and should not be treated as ordinary ventilation hygiene.

Can a report cover inaccessible ductwork?

Access limitations need recording. Additional access or investigation may be required before conclusions can be drawn about concealed sections.
