01 · Scope and preparation

Set a useful boundary

Define whether the enquiry covers one building, a tenant area or several sites. Include available bills, meter data, hours of use and information about major equipment. State any known changes that make one period difficult to compare with another.

02 · Scope and preparation

Look at operation alongside equipment

An audit can investigate operating patterns, controls, heating and cooling interaction, lighting and other significant loads. The agreed scope determines the level of site investigation and analysis. Missing meter or asset information should be identified at the start.

03 · Scope and preparation

Prioritise actions with clear assumptions

The findings can separate operational changes from measures requiring investment or further investigation. Any estimate depends on the evidence, tariffs and assumptions used. An audit does not replace a commercial EPC, statutory air-conditioning inspection or an ESOS assessment where one is required.

Before you enquire

Commercial Energy Audits: useful answers

Is this the same as an EPC?

No. An EPC is a standardised building rating. An audit focuses on energy use and improvement opportunities within the agreed scope.

Do I need interval meter data?

It can help, but send what you have. The available data and any limitations should inform the quotation.

Does an audit automatically satisfy ESOS?

No. ESOS has organisation-level requirements and a specific compliance process. Tell us if that is the purpose of the enquiry.
