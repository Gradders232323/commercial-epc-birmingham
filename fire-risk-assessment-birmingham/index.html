Existing commercial premises

When this service can help

  • Responsible persons reviewing an existing workplace
  • Managing agents coordinating shared escape routes
  • Operators responding to changes in use or layout

01 · Scope and preparation

Set the premises and responsibility boundaries

Identify the floors or units included, the occupiers and the person coordinating fire-safety arrangements. Shared routes or systems may require cooperation between responsible persons. A single postal address does not necessarily establish one complete assessment boundary.

02 · Scope and preparation

Provide evidence of the current arrangements

Send the previous assessment, available plans and records for relevant fire precautions. Explain changes in layout, staffing, storage or occupation and identify known concerns. Access is needed to understand the agreed areas; restrictions should be recorded rather than overlooked.

03 · Scope and preparation

Use the findings as part of ongoing management

The written assessment should identify hazards, evaluate risk and support appropriate actions. It is not a blanket compliance certificate, nor does it automatically include testing of alarms, emergency lighting or electrical installations. The responsible person must manage the findings and keep the assessment under review as circumstances require.

Before you enquire

Fire Risk Assessments: useful answers

Does an assessment include alarm or lighting tests?

Not by default. A review of arrangements and specialist testing are different scopes.

Can a multi-occupied property be assessed?

Describe the occupations, common areas and responsible persons so the boundaries and cooperation requirements can be agreed.

Is one visit a guarantee of compliance?

No. The findings support decisions and ongoing management. Actions, maintenance and changing conditions still need attention.
