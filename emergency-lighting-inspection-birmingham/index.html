01 · Scope and preparation

Start with the system and the fire-safety context

Identify maintained or non-maintained equipment where known, the areas served and the person managing the testing. The fire risk assessment can help explain the role of emergency lighting in the premises. An inspection does not itself establish a complete fire-safety strategy.

02 · Scope and preparation

Agree the inspection and testing method

Routine functional checks, duration testing and a review of design or coverage are not interchangeable. Specify which is needed and provide existing drawings and records. Testing must be planned so occupied escape routes are not left without appropriate protection while equipment recovers.

03 · Scope and preparation

Record findings and agree corrective work

The report should identify the scope, findings and relevant limitations. Battery replacements, repairs, additional luminaires and design changes should be agreed separately. Frequencies and procedures must follow the system, risk assessment and applicable guidance rather than an invented universal timetable.

Birmingham property context

Coordinate testing with business operations

For occupied Birmingham premises, identify critical equipment, opening hours and any isolation restrictions. Agree who can authorise access or interruptions and which installation or appliances are included in the inspection.

Before you enquire

Emergency Lighting Inspections: useful answers

Is this included in a fire risk assessment?

A fire risk assessment may review arrangements, but it does not automatically include electrical testing of emergency lighting. Define both requirements.

Can testing take place during occupation?

The visit needs planning around the system and premises. Explain opening hours and any areas that cannot be taken out of use.

Does a test prove the lighting design is adequate?

Not necessarily. Functional testing and verification of coverage or design are different scopes.
