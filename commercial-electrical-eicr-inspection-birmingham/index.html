An installation-specific scope with planned access

Tell us the property, its current use and the purpose of your enquiry. The appropriate scope, provider and arrangements are confirmed before work is agreed.

01 · Scope and preparation

Define the fixed installation and limitations

Identify the buildings, areas, distribution boards and circuits included. Supply plans, earlier reports and records of alterations where available. Restricted access and agreed limitations should be recorded rather than assuming every circuit can be fully examined during normal trading.

02 · Scope and preparation

Plan isolation and business continuity

Inspection and testing can require isolation or interruption to circuits. Explain critical equipment, alarms, IT loads, occupied areas and any out-of-hours requirement before a quotation is agreed. The work should be undertaken by a person competent for the installation and testing involved.

03 · Scope and preparation

Separate findings from remedial work

An EICR reports the condition found within its scope and uses classification codes for observations. Repairs, further investigation and subsequent confirmation of work should be specifically agreed. There is no single inspection interval that can safely be assumed for every commercial property.

Commercial Electrical EICR Inspections: useful answers

Does an EICR include portable appliance testing?

Not as a default. An EICR concerns the fixed installation; appliance inspection or testing is a separate scope.

Will there be a power interruption?

Testing may need isolation. Tell us about critical operations so the access and outage plan can be discussed.

Are repairs automatically included?

No. Remedial work and any further investigation should be separately defined in the quotation.
