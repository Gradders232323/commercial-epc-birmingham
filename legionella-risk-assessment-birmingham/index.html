Know the water system. Understand the risk.

Tell us the property, its current use and the purpose of your enquiry. The appropriate scope, provider and arrangements are confirmed before work is agreed.

01 · Scope and preparation

Identify the systems and use patterns

Provide information on storage, hot-water generation, showers and other relevant outlets or equipment. Explain low-use areas, vulnerable occupants where relevant to the assessment and any recent changes. A plan or schematic helps establish the extent of the review.

02 · Scope and preparation

Review evidence and management responsibilities

Include previous risk assessments, monitoring records, maintenance information and the person responsible for managing the system. The assessment needs to consider risk and existing controls together. Missing records should be flagged so the quotation reflects the available evidence.

03 · Scope and preparation

Agree proportionate follow-up

The findings can inform a control scheme, further investigation or changes to management arrangements. Sampling, cleaning, disinfection and remedial work are separate activities unless specifically included. An assessment is not a promise that water is universally safe or that every system needs a complex monitoring programme.

Before you enquire

Legionella Risk Assessments: useful answers

Is water sampling always part of the assessment?

No. Sampling needs a defined purpose and may not be necessary for every system. The proposed approach should follow the assessed circumstances.

Is a water-hygiene inspection the same service?

Not automatically. A broader condition or records review does not replace a Legionella risk assessment unless that scope is expressly included.

What if parts of the building have been unused?

Explain the period of low use and planned reopening so stagnation and management arrangements can be considered.
