Refrigerant information and equipment records first

Tell us the property, its current use and the purpose of your enquiry. The appropriate scope, provider and arrangements are confirmed before work is agreed.

01 · Scope and preparation

Identify the installation

Send the make, model, refrigerant designation, charge information and any existing logbook. Label photographs can help locate the relevant information. Include details of leak-detection equipment if present rather than assuming a standard arrangement.

02 · Scope and preparation

Confirm the appropriate check

The applicable requirements depend on the gas, equipment and charge information. Work involving regulated refrigerants must be undertaken by personnel with the relevant qualifications and, where required, an appropriately certified business. The correct provider and scope need confirmation before the work is booked.

03 · Scope and preparation

Keep repairs and inspection duties distinct

A leak-check enquiry is not an instruction to replace refrigerant, repair equipment or certify every aspect of the installation. Describe any known leak or recent repair so the required work and follow-up can be established. Routine servicing and TM44 energy inspections are separate matters.

F-Gas Leak Checks: useful answers

Is a TM44 report enough?

No. TM44 concerns energy efficiency. Refrigerant leak checks have a different purpose and regulatory basis.

Is there one inspection interval for all units?

No. The relevant requirements depend on the equipment and refrigerant information. Send the records for review.

Can repairs be included?

Describe the known problem. Repairs, any refrigerant work and follow-up requirements must be specifically agreed.
