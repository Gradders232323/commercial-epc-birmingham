01 · Scope and preparation

Review the rule that applies to the property

The current minimum standard for in-scope non-domestic rented property is EPC E, subject to the regulations and applicable exemptions. A review needs to consider whether the premises and tenancy fall within scope. Future policy proposals should not be treated as a rule already applying to the building.

02 · Scope and preparation

Separate improvements from exemptions

Provide the existing EPC, recommendation report, lease information and any registered exemption records. A poor rating does not automatically mean an exemption is available. Equally, an EPC alone does not establish the full legal position for a particular letting.

03 · Scope and preparation

A documented route to the next decision

The review can identify evidence gaps, rating improvement options and matters requiring specialist advice. An EPC reassessment, installation work or legal opinion may be separate from the initial assessment. No target rating or lawful letting outcome is guaranteed in advance.

MEES Compliance Assessments: useful answers

Does MEES apply to every building?

No. The non-domestic rented-property rules have a defined scope. Provide the property and letting details so applicability can be reviewed.

Can you guarantee an EPC E rating?

No. The rating depends on the building, services and supported assessment inputs. Any improvement scenario needs checking against the actual property.

Is an exemption automatic if works are expensive?

No. Exemptions have criteria and evidence requirements. The appropriate basis and registration position need review.
