Existing commercial premises

When this service can help

  • Landlords considering upgrades before remarketing
  • Owners reviewing a low commercial EPC rating
  • Agents comparing proposed lighting, heating or fabric changes

01 · Scope and preparation

Check the starting point

Supply the current EPC and recommendation report, plans and evidence of the installed services. Changes made since the assessment may need documenting. An improvement exercise is more useful when the existing building information is accurate and the intended outcome is clear.

02 · Scope and preparation

Compare measures as a building-specific package

Lighting, controls, heating, cooling and fabric measures can affect the assessment differently. A report should explain which options warrant further examination and what evidence supports the assumptions. A generic shopping list is not a prediction of the resulting EPC band.

03 · Scope and preparation

Keep the final certificate separate

A prospective improvement report is not a replacement EPC and does not itself change the public rating. Completed works, supporting evidence and any subsequent assessment need to be dealt with before a new certificate can be issued. Installation specifications and costs require their own review.

Before you enquire

EPC Improvement Reports: useful answers

Will the report change the registered rating?

No. It supports decisions about improvements. A new certificate requires the appropriate assessment and lodgement.

Can proposed works be compared?

Yes, describe the options and provide specifications where available. The quotation should define what comparison or modelling is included.

Are energy savings guaranteed?

No. An EPC rating and actual consumption are different measures. Occupancy, operation and weather also affect bills.
